# THE HAM-MASIR DESIGN CANON

*The constitution of Ham-Masir — why it exists, where it lives, how it is built, and what it refuses to become.*

**Version:** 1.0 · **Status:** Living document · **Audience:** designers, illustrators, writers, engineers, founders — and any AI agent building on this product.

---

## How to use this document

This canon has a companion. Keep them in their lanes:

| Document | Answers | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **This canon** | *Why* and *what for* | Philosophy, principles, refusals, decision tests |
| **`docs/brand.md`** | *How*, exactly | Color tokens, type scale, spacing, motion values, do/don't lists |

When you need a hex code, open `brand.md`. When you need to decide whether a feature *belongs*, open this.

**Appendix A resolves the contradictions inside the original eight volumes.** Read it before you cite any single volume as authority. **Appendix B lists what is still undecided** — do not invent answers to those; escalate.

## Part 0 — The canon on one page

1. **Companionship, not instruction.** A teacher stands above you. An expert stands apart. A companion walks beside you.
2. **The relationship is the hero** — never the mentor, never the student, never the software.
3. **The real product is confidence**, created when uncertainty meets experience. Everything else is infrastructure.
4. **Calm is the antidote, not denial.** We never pretend the pressure isn't there; we make it lighter to carry.
5. **Progress is continuity, not speed.** Walking slowly in the right direction is progress.
6. **Space is trust.** Whitespace, silence, and patience are content.
7. **Two is the natural state.** Parallel, never merged — a constant gap that means respect.
8. **Trust over engagement.** If someone leaves because they found clarity, we succeeded.
9. **Nothing competitive.** No trophies, streaks, leaderboards, finish lines, manufactured urgency.
10. **The software should slowly become unnecessary.** The highest compliment is *"we still talk."*

# Part I — The Soul

### Why Ham-Masir exists

People rarely fail because information is unavailable. They fail because they walk difficult paths alone.

Courses are everywhere. Books are everywhere. AI explains almost anything. Yet students still feel lost — not because knowledge is scarce, but because **experience is invisible**. Every year thousands repeat mistakes someone else made three years earlier. The tragedy is not a lack of knowledge; it is a lack of *accessible* experience.

Experience already exists. It simply isn't organized, discoverable, or shared naturally. Ham-Masir exists to change that.

### The origin sentence

No search engine, video, or model can replace hearing another human say:

> *"I remember this part. I've been here before. Walk with me."*

That sentence is the origin of Ham-Masir. Everything else exists to support it.

### The real product

Not a mentorship platform. Not a marketplace. Not an educational startup.

**We build confidence.** Confidence is created the moment someone realizes another person has already survived what they are experiencing. That moment is the product. Everything else is infrastructure.

### The invisible problem

The hardest moments in education rarely happen inside classrooms. They happen afterwards — walking back to the dorm, looking at unfinished assignments, wondering whether changing majors is a mistake, feeling behind, feeling like everyone else already understands.

Traditional educational products optimize learning. **Ham-Masir optimizes those invisible moments.**

### Emotional principles

Every decision should increase at least one of: **trust · relief · belonging · hope · curiosity · encouragement · calmness · confidence.**

Never: excitement, urgency, competition. **We never create anxiety to increase engagement.** Attention is not the goal. Trust is.

### The precise meaning of "companion"

The word is overused in software. Here it has one definition:

> A companion is someone who reduces the weight of a journey simply by choosing to walk beside another person.

They do not remove obstacles. They do not promise success. They do not guarantee outcomes. They make uncertainty easier to carry. **That is enough.**

### What success looks like

Not completed sessions. Not revenue. Not active users. The deepest success is a student finishing a conversation thinking: *"I feel lighter than I did an hour ago."*

### The brand promise

We never promise certainty, perfect outcomes, or shortcuts. We promise something quieter: **whatever path you are walking, you do not have to walk it alone.**

# Part II — The World

### It is a place, not an interface

People don't remember interfaces. They remember places — the café where they had an important conversation, the library where they stayed until midnight, the bench where someone gave them confidence.

Ham-Masir should feel like one of those places. Interfaces disappear; **atmosphere remains**.

### The emotional climate

**Quiet optimism** — believing tomorrow can be better without pretending today is perfect. This climate lives in illustrations, motion, copy, empty states, onboarding, and error messages alike.

### The recurring environments

- **The campus** — chosen not because universities define learning, but because campuses represent *transition*. People arrive uncertain and leave transformed. Long pathways, morning light, stone paths softened by footsteps. Nothing rushed.
- **The library** — a sanctuary, not a warehouse. Silence is respect, not emptiness. Warm wood, daylight, communal tables. Books present; people more important.
- **The café** — the symbol of equality. No podium, no hierarchy. Two people sharing a table, staying longer than necessary because they enjoy being there.
- **The path** — the central symbol. Almost every environment contains one: a walkway, a staircase, a bridge, a trail. It reminds us that every person is somewhere between beginning and becoming.

### Nature, light, materials

**Nature is companionship, not decoration.** Trees accompany people; sunlight accompanies architecture. The world breathes slowly — no dramatic storms, no violent landscapes.

**Light is emotional:** morning = possibility · afternoon = conversation · golden hour = reflection · overcast = honesty. Avoid theatrical contrast and darkness used for effect.

**Materials are tactile and lived-in:** wood, stone, paper, cotton, glass, ceramic, fabric. Never glossy plastic, chrome, or futuristic surfaces. Nothing disposable.

### Objects and time

Objects tell stories: a notebook filled with handwriting, two coffee cups, a backpack against a chair, a worn textbook. **Nothing staged** — every object suggests someone was just here.

Ham-Masir lives in **ordinary time**. It celebrates Tuesday afternoons more than graduation ceremonies. Ordinary days build extraordinary lives.

### What this world never becomes

A futuristic smart city · a corporate headquarters · a luxury university · a gaming environment · a cyberpunk landscape · a social-media aesthetic · a neon startup office · a perfect utopia.

**Imperfection makes places believable. Believability creates attachment.**

### The feeling of arrival

Opening Ham-Masir for the first time should not feel like entering a platform. It should feel like arriving somewhere you always hoped existed — a place where asking questions is normal, uncertainty is respected, and people remember what it felt like to be beginners.

# Part III — Companion Geometry

*Swiss design was built on the grid. Material on physical metaphor. Ham-Masir is built on **relationships**.*

Geometry here is not decoration — it is behavior made visible. A circle is a person. A line is movement. The distance between two lines is trust. Curves are willingness.

**If the logo disappeared entirely, the product should still feel unmistakably like Ham-Masir.** That is the purpose of this part.

### The eight principles

**I. Two is the natural state.** The universe of Ham-Masir rarely begins with one. Two paths, two people, two voices. Two is not repetition — it is dialogue. When a design feels lonely, ask whether something should quietly accompany it.

**II. Parallel without dependency.** Companions travel together but remain independent. Neither consumes, dominates, or disappears into the other. The relationship is voluntary.

**III. Constant trust.** The gap between companions is intentional and consistent. Trust has rhythm; inconsistency creates uncertainty. The user may never consciously notice. Their subconscious will.

**IV. Curves before angles.** Life rarely changes direction instantly. Curves communicate adaptation; sharp angles communicate conflict. Corners soften, paths flow, interactions ease. Nothing abrupt.

**V. The space between.** Empty space is where relationships become visible. A crowded interface isn't just overwhelming — it is *emotionally impatient*. Ham-Masir always chooses patience.

**VI. Forward motion.** Even still compositions imply continuation: a path leaving the frame, a staircase disappearing upward. Movement suggests **possibility, never speed**.

**VII. Gravity toward humanity.** Every composition guides attention toward people — not technology, not decoration, not interface chrome.

**VIII. Gentle alignment.** Perfect symmetry feels institutional. Prefer balance. Small imperfections create warmth.

### Applied geometry

- **Shape vocabulary (deliberately small):** rounded rectangles · circles · continuous curves · soft arcs · open-ended paths. Every additional geometric language weakens the identity.
- **Corners:** rounded, never childish — as if every surface has been gently worn by years of human use. **No 0-radius corners.**
- **Grid & rhythm:** generous spacing, breathing margins, no density. Hierarchy should feel *discovered*, not announced. Design breathes the way people do.
- **Icons:** tiny stories, not symbols. Rounded strokes, open forms, minimal intersections, consistent weight — **as though drawn with the same pen that drew the logo.**
- **Motion:** natural acceleration, gentle deceleration, no overshoot, no bounce, no competition for attention. Movement reassures; it does not entertain.

### The five-question screen test

Before any screen ships:

1. Does this design feel *accompanied*?
2. Does it breathe?
3. Could any element be removed without changing the emotional message?
4. Does movement feel natural rather than mechanical?
5. Would someone unfamiliar with the logo still recognize this as Ham-Masir?

Any "no" means the work is unfinished.

# Part IV — The Illustration Bible

### What we illustrate

Most educational products illustrate knowledge: books, certificates, graduation caps, light bulbs. **We illustrate people** — because the platform is not about learning, it is about *learning together*. Every illustration begins with a human story, never an object, never an interface.

### The hero rule

The hero is never an individual — never the mentor, never the student, never success. **The hero is the invisible connection between two people.** Test: if one person were removed and the emotional meaning survived, the illustration has failed.

### Moments, not events

Marketing loves events — graduations, awards, launches. We draw **moments**: a student hesitating before asking a question; someone opening a notebook after a difficult day; a mentor patiently listening; a quiet conversation while walking across campus.

> Moments become memories. Events become advertisements. Ham-Masir collects memories.

### Characters

Ordinary people. Nobody idealized, nobody extraordinary, nobody an icon. Expressions stay subtle — relief, curiosity, warmth, concentration, hope. **Real confidence is quiet.** No theatrical smiles, no exaggerated gestures, no pointing excitedly at invisible interfaces.

Clothing is ordinary: hoodies, sweaters, jackets, backpacks, sneakers. Nothing that distracts from the relationship.

### Composition and camera

Every composition begins with breathing room; characters never fill every corner. **The viewer is never an observer from above** — the camera stands beside the characters at human eye level, participating in the conversation rather than documenting it.

### Storytelling test

Every illustration answers three questions: *Who are these people? What are they experiencing? How has the relationship changed by the end of the moment?* If it can't, it contains decoration instead of narrative.

### Illustration families (organize by emotion, not by feature)

**Beginning** — first day, choosing, searching, asking · **Growing** — studying, practicing, trying again · **Belonging** — friendships, shared experiences, group work · **Guidance** — listening, explaining, walking together, planning · **Reflection** — looking back, writing notes, thinking quietly · **Achievement** — not trophies: peace, confidence, a smile after understanding, returning to help someone else.

### Empty states as illustrations

Absence still carries hope. *No mentors found* → someone waiting patiently beside a path. *No conversations* → an empty bench with two cups. *No bookings* → a calendar beside a resting backpack.

### What we never draw

Pressure · competition · leaderboards · victory poses · explosions · fireworks · holograms · floating UI panels · people jumping in the air · artificial happiness · corporate handshakes · technology worship.

### The memory test

> *"Could someone look at this image ten years from now and remember a person rather than a product?"*

If they only remember the visual style, the illustration has failed.

# Part V — Product Philosophy

### Before asking what a feature does, ask what it should make people feel

Every feature exists to support a relationship **between two people** — never between a person and the software. The software should become *increasingly invisible* as trust between people grows. That is the measure of good product design here.

### Every screen is part of a journey

No page exists independently. Each answers *"Where is this person on their journey?"* — not *"Which feature are they using?"*

- The dashboard is not a dashboard. It is **a place to continue.**
- Search is not search. It is **the beginning of trust.**
- A profile is not a résumé. It is **someone's story.**
- Booking is not scheduling. It is **making a commitment.**
- Chat is not messaging. It is **companionship unfolding.**

### Search

Traditional products help users find *results*. We help them find *people*. Search should not feel like filtering products; it should feel like discovering someone who understands. Where possible, questions replace filters and conversation replaces configuration. *(See Appendix A.4 — this coexists with, rather than replaces, structured filtering.)*

### Profiles

A profile is a narrative, not a biography. Users should understand **who someone is** before **what they achieved**. Every profile answers: Who are you? What path have you already walked? What did that journey teach you? Who might benefit from your experience?

Credentials matter. Humanity matters more.

### Reviews

The most valuable review is emotional, not numerical. Ask *"How did this conversation help you move forward?"* rather than only *"How many stars?"* Future students should understand how someone made another person **feel** — not merely whether they were punctual. *(See Appendix A.3 for how this coexists with ratings.)*

### Booking, conversation, waiting

**Booking is anticipation, not administration** — the first shared commitment between two people. Language matters: prefer *"a time that works for both of you"* over *"select time slot."*

**Chat is a room**, not a support desk: two comfortable chairs, a table, silence when needed. Read receipts must never create pressure; typing indicators reassure rather than rush. Everything encourages presence; nothing encourages addiction.

**Waiting is emotional.** Someone waiting for a reply imagines the worst. Fill waiting with context — people are patient when uncertainty disappears.

### Dashboard, notifications, empty states, errors

- **Dashboard** celebrates *continuity*, not metrics: where you are, who you're walking with, what's next, how far you've come. Never judged — only accompanied.
- **Notifications** must pass one test: *"Would I say this to someone sitting beside me?"* If not, don't send it. **Silence is often the correct design decision.**
- **Empty states** are beginnings, not failures. Not *"no results found"* but *"we haven't found the right companion yet."*
- **Errors** never blame. People already blame themselves enough. Acknowledge the problem without assigning responsibility.
- **Success is quiet.** A completed booking deserves reassurance, not fireworks.

### Time, pricing, growth

**Time feels human** — avoid countdowns and urgency; never manipulate.

**Pricing is respectful** — never luxurious, never cheap, never aggressive. People are paying to honor another person's willingness to share experience.

**Growth is never gamified** — no streaks, no meaningless badges, no artificial urgency. Recognize progress; never manufacture it.

### Trust over engagement

Time spent inside the application is not success. Time *well* spent is. If a user leaves because they found clarity, the product succeeded.

The greatest compliment is not *"the UI is beautiful"* or *"the platform is useful."* It is: **"we still talk."** Software should create relationships strong enough to eventually make itself less necessary. Very few products aspire to disappear. Ham-Masir should.

### The final question before shipping anything

> **Does this feature help people walk together, or does it simply help them use software?**

If the second — keep designing.

# Part VI — Interface Principles

A design system is not a component library. Components are the *consequence* of decisions about human behavior.

Before *how a button looks*, ask **what pressing it should feel like**. Before a card's radius, ask **why the card exists**. Before an animation, ask **what emotion remains after it finishes**.

### Calm before clever

Predictability creates confidence → confidence creates trust → trust creates belonging. A user should never wonder how the interface behaves; their attention belongs elsewhere.

### Component intent

| Component | Emotional purpose | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Buttons** | Invitations, never commands | Encourage movement; never pressure. See A.5 on wording. |
| **Primary actions** | Commitment | Weight without shouting. No oversized buttons, heavy shadows, or aggressive gradients. *Confidence never raises its voice.* |
| **Secondary actions** | Respect for autonomy | Stay visible, not hidden or disabled by default. Choice communicates respect. |
| **Cards** | Introductions, not containers | Every card is a person or an opportunity. *A card should feel like receiving a letter, not opening a spreadsheet.* |
| **Lists** | Discovery, not browsing | Humans don't enjoy browsing people. Limit cognitive load; quality before quantity. |
| **Forms** | Conversations | Every input asks for trust. Ask only what matters, explain why. Clearer beats shorter. |
| **Validation** | Encouragement | *"We couldn't understand this yet"* — never *"invalid field."* |
| **Errors** | Kindness | Errors belong to systems, not people: *"We couldn't verify those details."* |
| **Loading** | Waiting together | Show progress, give context, reduce uncertainty. |
| **Navigation** | Orientation | Always know where you are, where you came from, where you can go. Disorientation is anxiety. |
| **Typography** | A calm person speaking clearly | Never corporate, decorative, or dramatic. Comfortable line heights. |
| **White space** | Respect | Every margin says *"take your time."* |
| **Motion** | Explanation | Answers *"what just changed?"* If it exists only to impress, remove it. |
| **Color** | Hierarchy, not personality | Restraint. Reserve emphasis for meaningful moments. |
| **Icons** | Drawn, not engineered | One family, no stylistic exceptions. |
| **Notifications** | A borrowed moment | Interruption is a privilege. |
| **Success messages** | Quiet celebration | *Confidence whispers.* |

### Accessibility

Accessibility is **not compliance — it is companionship**. If someone cannot comfortably use the interface, the philosophy has already been broken.

### Responsiveness

Desktop offers space · tablet offers flexibility · mobile offers intimacy. **The philosophy never changes; only the canvas does.**

### The interface test

Does it reduce uncertainty? Respect attention? Encourage rather than pressure? Leave room to breathe? Help people focus on each other instead of the software?

Any "no" — keep designing.

# Part VII — Beyond the Product

### Culture, not just platform

A product solves a problem. A brand creates recognition. **A culture changes behavior.** The moment Ham-Masir becomes only software, it has become smaller than its purpose. Its purpose is to *normalize companionship*.

The philosophy must survive any technological evolution. If one day there are no screens at all, it should still make sense — because Ham-Masir is ultimately a human behavior, not a digital artifact.

### The cycle

Every student eventually becomes a guide. Every guide was once a beginner. Nobody remains only a learner; nobody remains only a mentor. **Receiving guidance naturally becomes giving guidance** — not because the platform asks, but because gratitude evolves into generosity.

*(This cycle is also encoded in the logo: the companion of today becomes the leader of tomorrow.)*

### How the philosophy shows up off-screen

- **Events** are built around conversations, not speakers. Tables replace auditoriums; circles grow as stages shrink.
- **Physical spaces** embody what the digital space promises: warm wood, natural light, shared tables, no giant branding.
- **Objects** are companions, not merchandise — a notebook, a bookmark, a canvas bag. Useful enough to stay in someone's daily life. Branding subtle, meaning obvious.
- **Writing** encourages, never advertises. One human speaking to another, never a company speaking to an audience.
- **Social media** is a public notebook — stories, lessons, failures, reflections. Depth over reach. Every post should leave people thinking rather than scrolling.
- **Photography** documents, never performs. Imperfection creates credibility; staged perfection creates distance.
- **Partnerships** are chosen for shared philosophy, not shared market.
- **Growth** is organic. Word of mouth is evidence that trust exists. **Design for recommendation, not virality.**

### Internal culture

**Leadership resembles mentorship** — transparent, patient, curious. Founders behave exactly as the platform asks mentors to behave. Culture begins internally; it cannot be performed externally.

**Hire for alignment before skill.** Skills can be developed; philosophy is much harder to teach.

**Scale should increase reach, never distance.** Large organizations become efficient. Ham-Masir must remain personal.

### Legacy

Twenty years from now: a graduate helps a first-year student, who later helps another, who later helps another — and none of them remember where the tradition began. **That is success. The philosophy has escaped the product and entered culture.**

# Part VIII — The Anti-Canon

*Brands rarely lose their identity because they forget what they wanted to build. They lose it because they forget what they promised never to become.*

**These refusals outrank every other part of this document.** When a growth idea conflicts with one of them, the refusal wins.

### The refusals

**We refuse to build addiction.** No infinite feeds, variable rewards, endless scrolling, or manipulation disguised as engagement. If the healthiest outcome is for someone to close the app and continue living, that is success.

**We refuse to turn people into metrics.** MAU, session length, retention, conversion — these describe scale. Stories describe impact. One student finding one life-changing mentor outweighs a thousand meaningless interactions.

**We refuse to manufacture urgency.** No *"only three spots left,"* no *"someone else is viewing this profile."* Real opportunities are meaningful without artificial pressure. Trust grows slowly; we respect that pace.

**We refuse to gamify human relationships.** No streaks, no trophies, no experience points. Helping someone should never feel like farming. The reward is the relationship.

**We refuse to celebrate busyness.** A mentor with hundreds of sessions is not automatically better; a student studying twelve hours a day is not automatically healthier. Rest is part of learning. Silence is part of conversation.

**We refuse to replace humans with technology.** AI may reduce friction and prepare conversations — it must never *become* the conversation. Whenever technology threatens to replace human connection, **the human connection wins. Every time.**

**We refuse to build a personal-brand platform.** Mentors are not influencers. The loudest voice is rarely the most helpful. Reward generosity over visibility, humility over performance, depth over audience size.

**We refuse to create educational elitism.** No university makes someone inherently wiser. The student who struggled and eventually succeeded often has more to teach than the one for whom everything came easily. **We value lived experience above prestige.** *(See Appendix A.6.)*

**We refuse to sell insecurity.** We will never say *you are behind, you are missing out, you are not enough.* Our role is to reduce anxiety, not create it. Confidence built on fear collapses; confidence built on companionship endures.

**We refuse to chase trends.** We may learn from them; we will never depend on them. Fashion expires; character remains.

**We refuse to confuse growth with scale.** Every new feature, country, language, and mentor should make the experience richer — not noisier. Growth that weakens intimacy is expansion without direction.

**We refuse to interrupt without purpose.** Every notification borrows a moment from someone's life. **Silence is a feature.**

**We refuse to forget beginners.** Mature products drift toward experts and beginners become invisible. If the product becomes intimidating, it has forgotten its own origin.

**We refuse to worship efficiency.** Sometimes a slower conversation is more valuable than a faster solution. We optimize only when optimization serves people.

**We refuse to forget why we started.** The greatest danger is not failure — it is comfort. Every major decision begins with: **"Would the first version of Ham-Masir still recognize us?"**

### The Oath

Everyone who contributes — designer, engineer, writer, researcher, mentor, founder — should be able to stand behind this:

> We will never manipulate where we can encourage.
> We will never pressure where we can reassure.
> We will never replace people where we can connect them.
> We will never optimize attention at the expense of trust.
> We will never forget that every screen we design exists between two human beings.

### The last sentence

Ham-Masir does not exist to protect software, a logo, or a business model. It exists to protect one timeless act of generosity:

> Someone who has already walked the road choosing to walk it once again — simply so another person doesn't have to walk it alone.

That is not our feature. That is our legacy.

# Appendix A — Resolved contradictions

*The original volumes contain real conflicts, both internally and with shipped product decisions. These are the rulings. Cite this appendix, not the raw volume, when they disagree.*

### A.1 — Color: navy is out

**Conflict:** Volumes II and IV list *"deep navy"* (and *"soft blues," "stone gray"*) among the world's colors. The locked brand palette from the identity study is **teal `#5B9E94` + peach `#E8B99A` + cream**, with no navy.

**Ruling:** **No navy, no blue.** The "stability and trust" role belongs to the primary teal; the "deep anchor" role belongs to the warm ink `#2C3A38`. Volumes II and IV predate the palette lock — treat their color lines as superseded by `brand.md §1`. Warm neutrals (stone, sand) are permitted as illustration support only.

### A.2 — "We are not a marketplace" vs. what we actually built

**Conflict:** Volume V opens with *"Ham-Masir is not a marketplace,"* while the product has listings, filters, prices, ratings, bookings, and a commission.

**Ruling:** Both are true and the distinction is operational, not rhetorical. **We use marketplace mechanisms; we refuse marketplace incentives.** Concretely:

- ✅ Allowed: listings, search, prices, scheduling, payment, reviews.
- ❌ Refused: urgency mechanics, "X people viewing," ranking mentors by revenue, upsell pressure, engagement optimization, treating a booking as the end of the funnel.

Rule of thumb: **a mechanism is fine; an incentive that makes the transaction more important than the relationship is not.**

### A.3 — Reviews: "never scores" vs. the rating on the mentor card

**Conflict:** Volume V says reviews should never become scores, because scores create comparison and comparison creates competition. The shipped mentor card shows a star rating and review count — deliberately, because a stressed student choosing a stranger needs a fast credibility signal.

**Ruling:** **Both, with different weights at different moments.**

- **At the choosing moment** (results card): a rating is legitimate and required. It reduces anxiety and speeds a hard decision. Keep it calm and small — a signal, never a scoreboard.
- **At the reading moment** (profile / booking sheet): the *written*, emotional review leads. The number supports it; it never headlines it.
- **Never:** rank mentors publicly by score, show "top rated" leaderboards, badge anyone as best, or let a low score visibly shame a mentor.

The refusal was against *competition between mentors*, not against *reassurance for students*.

### A.4 — Search: "questions replace filters" vs. the filter screen we built

**Conflict:** Volumes V and VI say search should begin with empathy and questions rather than filters. The shipped discovery screen is entirely filters.

**Ruling:** **Two doors, one destination.**

- **Structured filtering stays.** It is fast, respectful of people who already know what they want, and it is honest.
- **A guided mode is owed.** A short, warm, question-led path ("چه چیزی این روزها بیشتر اذیتت می‌کنه؟") that produces the same results. This is a **committed roadmap item**, not optional flavor — without it, Volume V's promise is unfulfilled.
- Filters must never *feel* like filtering products: label them in human terms, and always show a live count so the screen answers back.

### A.5 — Button copy: poetry vs. clarity

**Conflict:** Volume VI proposes *"Walk Together"* instead of *"Book."* In production this risks ambiguity — and ambiguity is the opposite of calm.

**Ruling:** **Clarity outranks poetry on any action the user must understand to proceed.** The button says what happens. Warmth lives in the surrounding copy, the plan titles, and the confirmation — where it costs nothing.

- ✅ `رزرو` / `ادامه به پرداخت` (clear action) + *"یک ساعت با کسی که راهِ تو را رفته"* (warm framing beside it).
- ❌ A primary button whose label leaves the user guessing what it does.

Accessibility reinforces this: screen-reader users and anxious users both need unambiguous labels.

### A.6 — "No educational elitism" vs. showing rank and university

**Conflict:** Volume VIII refuses elitism and values lived experience above prestige. The mentor card foregrounds konkur rank and university — which are prestige signals.

**Ruling:** Rank and university stay, because in this specific market they are the fastest proof that *this person actually walked your exact path* — that is **evidence of shared experience**, not a claim of superiority. But we hold three lines:

- Never sort, tier, or price mentors by prestige, and never label anyone "top."
- Never let a less prestigious mentor's card look visually weaker.
- The narrative (the story, "what that journey taught me") must carry at least as much visual weight as the credential in the profile and booking sheet.

If prestige ever starts driving discovery ranking, we have broken the refusal.

### A.7 — Illustration diversity vs. the same-gender pairing rule

**Conflict:** Volume IV calls for natural diversity including *"different genders"* in scenes. The settled platform rule is that **mentor↔student pairs are always same-gender**.

**Ruling:** The same-gender pairing rule holds for **any illustration depicting a mentor–student pair**, for cultural fit and user comfort in this market. Diversity is expressed across the *library as a whole* — both boy-with-boy and girl-with-girl pairs appear equally, across different fields, cities, and personalities — and freely within group or ambient scenes. Diversity across the set, not within the pair.

### A.8 — Volume VII and VIII overlap

Volume VII's *"The Brand We Refuse to Become"* duplicates Volume VIII. **Part VIII is the single authoritative list of refusals**; the VII section has been folded in.

# Appendix B — Open questions (do not answer these unilaterally)

These are unresolved and require a founder decision. Documented so they are not silently decided by whoever builds the next screen.

1. **The data business vs. the refusals.** The stated plan to sell student data to prep institutions sits in direct tension with *"we refuse to turn people into metrics"* and *"we refuse to sell insecurity"* — and many konkur students are minors. The safer resolution on the table (**aggregated, anonymized market insight + an explicit opt-in channel**, never individual data) has not been formally adopted. **Until it is, no data-sale feature should be designed or built.**

2. **Where the commission is stated.** The canon says pricing must feel respectful and money must never become the emotional center — but the platform's cut, and how transparently it is shown to mentors and students, is undecided.

3. **The Persian voice.** This canon defines tone in English abstractions. The operative product is Persian. A short Persian voice guide — sentence rhythm, formality level (رسمی vs. صمیمی), how we address a teenager without condescension — is missing and should be written as a companion to `brand.md §8`.

4. **The mentor's side of the philosophy.** Six of the eight volumes are written from the student's perspective. The mentor is a paid worker with limited time who can burn out. A companion doctrine for the mentor experience — fair load, protection from pressure, dignity in the panel — is not yet written.

5. **What happens when a relationship fails.** The canon has no guidance for a mentor who disappears, a mismatch, a refund, or a student who quits. These are exactly the moments where a companionship brand is most tested and most easily betrayed.

6. **Measurement.** *"I feel lighter than I did an hour ago"* is the stated success metric, but nothing defines how it is observed without turning people into metrics.
